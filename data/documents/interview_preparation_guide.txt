INTERVIEW PREPARATION GUIDE — INDUSTRY BEST PRACTICES
=======================================================

SECTION 1: THE STAR METHOD
----------------------------
The STAR method is the gold standard for answering behavioral interview questions.

- Situation: Set the scene and context. Be specific about when and where.
- Task: Describe the challenge or responsibility you faced.
- Action: Explain EXACTLY what YOU did (use "I", not "we").
- Result: Share the measurable outcome. Quantify whenever possible.

Example behavioral question: "Tell me about a time you had to meet a tight deadline."

STAR Answer Example:
Situation: Two weeks before a major product launch, our lead developer left unexpectedly.
Task: I had to complete the remaining 30% of backend development and ensure launch readiness.
Action: I reprioritized all non-critical features, worked closely with QA to define a reduced MVP scope, and brought in a contractor for frontend work. I held daily 15-minute standups to track progress.
Result: We launched on schedule with 95% of planned features. The product achieved 10,000 sign-ups in the first week.


SECTION 2: EXPERIENCE LEVEL EXPECTATIONS
------------------------------------------

FRESHER (0-1 years):
- Focus: Foundational knowledge, academic projects, internships, learning agility
- Interviewers expect: Basic CS concepts, enthusiasm, problem-solving approach
- Key areas: Data structures, algorithms, OOP fundamentals, one technology stack

JUNIOR (1-3 years):
- Focus: Hands-on coding ability, understanding of software development lifecycle
- Interviewers expect: Working independently on features, basic system design awareness
- Key areas: Code quality, debugging, testing, version control (Git), API design

MID-LEVEL (3-6 years):
- Focus: Ownership of features end-to-end, some technical leadership
- Interviewers expect: System design basics, mentoring juniors, cross-functional collaboration
- Key areas: System design, scalability, performance, code reviews, agile practices

SENIOR (6-10 years):
- Focus: Architecture decisions, leading teams, defining technical strategy
- Interviewers expect: Ambiguity handling, trade-off analysis, organizational impact
- Key areas: Distributed systems, leadership, stakeholder management, technical roadmap

LEAD / PRINCIPAL (10+ years):
- Focus: Org-wide technical vision, hiring decisions, long-term architecture
- Interviewers expect: Strategic thinking, cross-team alignment, industry-level thinking
- Key areas: Engineering culture, platform design, build vs. buy decisions, EM collaboration


SECTION 3: COMMON INTERVIEW FORMATS
-------------------------------------

1. PHONE SCREEN (20-30 min)
   - Goal: Basic qualification check
   - Typical questions: Background, motivation, 1-2 technical questions
   - Tip: Have your resume in front of you. Be concise.

2. TECHNICAL CODING ROUND (45-60 min)
   - Goal: Assess problem-solving and coding skills
   - Tools: LeetCode-style problems, live coding (CoderPad, HackerRank)
   - Tip: Think out loud. Clarify constraints before coding. Discuss time/space complexity.

3. SYSTEM DESIGN ROUND (45-60 min)
   - Goal: Assess architectural thinking (Mid-Level and above)
   - Topics: URL shortener, rate limiter, social feed, distributed cache
   - Tip: Use the RESHADED framework: Requirements, Estimation, Storage, High-level design, APIs, Detailed design, Evaluation, Distinguishing features.

4. BEHAVIORAL ROUND (30-45 min)
   - Goal: Assess culture fit, soft skills, leadership
   - Tip: Prepare 6-8 STAR stories covering: leadership, failure, conflict, impact, collaboration, growth.

5. CASE STUDY / TAKE-HOME (varies)
   - Goal: Assess practical skills and judgment
   - Tip: Document your thought process. Quality > completeness.


SECTION 4: SALARY NEGOTIATION
-------------------------------
- Always research market rate before negotiating (use Glassdoor, Levels.fyi, LinkedIn Salary)
- Let the employer make the first offer whenever possible
- Counter with a specific number, not a range
- Negotiate total compensation: base salary, equity, signing bonus, PTO
- Script: "Based on my research and the value I bring to this role, I was expecting something closer to [X]. Is there flexibility to get there?"


SECTION 5: QUESTIONS TO ASK THE INTERVIEWER
---------------------------------------------
Always ask thoughtful questions — it signals genuine interest.

1. "What does success look like for this role in the first 90 days?"
2. "What are the biggest technical challenges the team is currently facing?"
3. "How does engineering collaborate with product and design here?"
4. "What opportunities are there for professional development and growth?"
5. "How is performance reviewed, and how are promotions decided?"
6. "What do you personally enjoy most about working here?"


SECTION 6: TECHNICAL ASSESSMENT TIPS
--------------------------------------
Coding Interviews:
- Clarify the problem before writing code
- Start with a brute-force solution, then optimize
- Test with edge cases: empty input, single element, max size
- Communicate your approach throughout

System Design Interviews:
- Align on functional and non-functional requirements upfront
- Estimate scale: users, requests per second, storage
- Start simple, then add complexity (caching, sharding, replication)
- Discuss trade-offs explicitly

Domain Knowledge:
- Review fundamentals of your primary programming language
- Understand the data structures and their time complexities
- Study common design patterns (Factory, Observer, Strategy, etc.)
- Know your databases: SQL vs NoSQL, indexing, transactions, ACID


SECTION 7: SOFT SKILLS ASSESSMENT
------------------------------------
Interviewers assess:
- Communication: Can you explain complex ideas clearly?
- Collaboration: Do you work well with others and resolve conflicts?
- Growth mindset: Do you learn from failures and seek feedback?
- Ownership: Do you take responsibility for outcomes?
- Adaptability: Can you navigate change and ambiguity?

Key phrases that signal strong soft skills:
- "I took ownership of..."
- "I collaborated with X team to..."
- "I learned from that failure by..."
- "I proactively communicated the risk..."
- "I sought feedback from my manager and implemented..."
